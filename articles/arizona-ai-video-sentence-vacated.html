Arizona Court Vacates Manslaughter Sentence Over AI Video of Dead Victim

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk

An Arizona man's 10-and-a-half-year manslaughter sentence has been thrown out in a case where a video generated by artificial intelligence portrayed the deceased victim addressing a judge before punishment was imposed.

In a decision released Wednesday, the Arizona Court of Appeals ruled that Gabriel Paul Horcasitas must be resentenced in the 2021 road-rage shooting death of 37-year-old Christopher Pelkey, because the AI video wasn't reliable. His manslaughter conviction stands — only the sentence was vacated.

The video

Before Horcasitas' sentencing in May 2025, Pelkey's family presented an AI-generated video that recreated Pelkey's appearance and voice, addressing his family, the court, and Horcasitas. The synthetic version spoke of forgiveness — words written by Pelkey's sister, Stacey Wales, who said she wanted to give her brother a voice in the case over his death.

"In another life, we probably could have been friends," the AI version of Pelkey said, according to reports. "I believe in forgiveness, and a God who forgives. I always have and I still do."

Maricopa County Superior Court Judge Todd Lang was visibly moved. "I loved that AI," he said from the bench. "I heard the forgiveness. I feel that that was genuine." He then handed down a sentence of 10.5 years — more than prosecutors had asked for.

The ruling

The three-judge appeals panel found the video crossed a line. It didn't reflect actual events and presented statements made in the footage as coming directly from the victim.

"Rather than document an event or recording a particular moment, the AI video presents a depiction of the victim and his thoughts created from the imaginings of the victim's sister," the panel wrote.

The court found the video was "not reliable" and a "fundamental error" that left the sentencing "fundamentally unfair." This is believed to be the first time an Arizona appellate court has addressed the admissibility of AI-generated victim depictions in a judicial proceeding.

What happens now

Horcasitas will be resentenced in a new hearing, where Pelkey's family has said they are prepared to participate. Wales said she was disappointed the video was cited as the reason for the vacatur: "There is a convicted murderer sitting in prison that has blankets and walls of protection around their rights. Where are the rights for the victim?"

The case raises a question the legal system is only beginning to confront: when AI can make the dead speak, who gets to decide what they say?
