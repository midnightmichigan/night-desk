Back to PlayStation After Years Away: The PS5 Accessories Worth Buying First

Coming back to PlayStation after years away is a specific kind of fun: a new console, a back catalog of games everybody else finished years ago (often cheap now), and a shelf's worth of accessories all claiming to be essential. Most of them aren't. Here's the honest order — what earns its money first, and what can wait until you've played for a month.

Buy first

A second controller. The DualSense is the controller everything on PS5 is built around — adaptive triggers and haptics are part of how the games feel, not a gimmick. A second one covers three real situations: a dead battery mid-session, a second player on the couch, and a spare while the first charges. If you only buy one accessory, it's this one.

→ DualSense wireless controllers

A charging dock. Two controllers and one USB cable is a rotation problem. A drop-in dock that charges both at once ends it for the price of a pizza, and the console setup stays tidy.

→ PS5 controller charging docks

A headset you can wear for two hours. Game audio is half the experience now, and late-night sessions in a house with sleeping kids make speakers a non-starter anyway. What matters: comfort over spectacle (lightweight, breathable ear cups), a clear mic if you play with friends, and wireless if the cable across the couch will annoy you — it will.

→ PS5 gaming headsets

Buy when the library grows

Storage. A handful of big games fills the internal drive faster than anyone expects. We wrote a full guide to the two kinds of PS5 storage (external archive drives vs. internal NVMe) — the short version: don't buy either until the "delete something to install something" moment arrives, then buy once, bigger than you think.

A media remote — only if the console doubles as the house streaming box and someone in the house hates using a game controller for TV. Cheap, genuinely useful, completely optional for gaming.

→ PS5 media remotes

Skip for now

  • Console covers and skins — decoration; buy them when you want them, not as "setup."
  • Cooling stands with fans — the console manages its own heat; a stand that just holds games is fine, the powered-cooling claims are marketing.
  • A second-hand "pro" controller before you know your games — the standard DualSense is the right starting point; upgrade only for a reason you can name.

The returning player's real advantage

Your backlog is other people's old news, which means it's on sale constantly. Spend the accessory money on the controller, the dock, and the headset; spend the game money slowly, on the discounted greatest-hits shelf. Six years away didn't cost you anything — it just made everything cheaper.
