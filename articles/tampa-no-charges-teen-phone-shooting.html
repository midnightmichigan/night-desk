No charges for Tampa man who fatally shot 15-year-old searching for his lost phone

A man in his mid-20s who fatally shot 15-year-old Heitor Amaral during an encounter at a Tampa apartment complex on Sept. 20 will not face criminal charges, the Tampa Police Department announced Thursday.

The State Attorney's Office made the determination after "a comprehensive review of the evidence," saying it could not "successfully refute a claim of self-defense" under Florida law, according to a TPD news release shared Oct. 1.

"We recognize the profound pain and concern this tragedy has caused for the family and friends of those involved, and for our entire community," police said, adding that they released the "established sequence of events" to "ensure full transparency."

What happened

Heitor, a high-school freshman, realized he had lost his cellphone on Sept. 20 and teamed up with two friends to find it using tracking technology, which pointed to the area of the 13000 block of River Willow Place, police said.

The group entered the gated community through an open gate and parked their truck in the middle of a roadway while searching. After being asked by a community member to move the truck, the teens went door-to-door asking residents if anyone had seen the phone.

When they got no answer at one front door, they entered the enclosed back patio and started banging on the sliding glass door, according to investigators.

Two accounts

Police say the resident — identified only as a man in his mid-20s — put on a mask and armed himself to scare the group off. Two of the boys fled; investigators say a "physical altercation" then broke out between Heitor and the resident. The man told investigators Heitor placed him in a headlock, and he fired a single round in fear for his life. Heitor died of his injuries at the scene.

The resident's identity has not been publicly released.

A witness disputes that version. Yessenia Gonzalez, whose 16-year-old son Julio was with Heitor that night, told the Tampa Bay Times there was no fight before the shooting and that all three teenagers were running away when the resident opened fire.

"I want the truth out there," Gonzalez said. "There was no fighting. These kids had no weapons."

Gonzalez relayed her son's description of the masked shooter: "My son said, 'I don't know what scared me more, the guy wearing a mask in his own house or the gun.'"

Context

Florida's stand-your-ground law places a high bar on prosecutors to disprove self-defense claims, which is why charges were declined, per the State Attorney's Office. The decision does not prevent a civil case by the family.

Heitor's family identified him to the Tampa Bay Times as the youngest of three children who had just started high school. A family fundraiser for funeral expenses and legal costs had raised more than $6,000 toward a $10,000 goal.
