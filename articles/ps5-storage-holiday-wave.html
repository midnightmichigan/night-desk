PS5 Storage, Round Two: Making Room for the Holiday Game Wave

Our first PS5 storage guide solved the basic problem: the stock drive fills up, and the fix is an internal NVMe expansion. This follow-up is about what happens next — the holiday wave. December is when the big games land as gifts, the launch titles are at their largest, and a single modern blockbuster can eat 100–200 GB by itself. A drive that felt roomy in October is full by Christmas night, and "delete something" is a bad present. Here is the round-two system.

Know the three tiers of PS5 storage

Not all storage does the same job on a PS5, and buying the wrong tier is the expensive mistake.

  • Internal NVMe SSD (M.2 2280, PCIe Gen4, with a heatsink) — the only expansion that plays PS5 games directly. This is where your active library lives. If you upgrade one thing, it's this, sized generously: the holiday wave is exactly the use case for going bigger than you think you need.
  • External USB drive — cannot play PS5 games, but stores them (moving a game back to internal storage is far faster than re-downloading it) and plays PS4 games directly. This is cold storage and your PS4 library's home.
  • The stock internal drive — keep it for the operating system and your current rotation. Treat it as the express lane, not the warehouse.

The holiday triage: what lives where

Before the gift games arrive, do one honest pass over the library:

  • Playing now or weekly: internal NVMe. No debate.
  • Finished, might replay someday: move to the external drive. "Someday" games are what cold storage exists for.
  • Finished, won't replay, free-to-play you bounced off: delete. You can always re-download; shelf space in the library menu is free, drive space is not.
  • PS4 games: external drive, played from there. They never needed the fast lane.

Captures are storage too

Game captures and clips quietly consume gigabytes, and the holiday season — new games, launch nights, first-play sessions — is peak capture season. Move the keepers off the console to a USB stick or external drive regularly, and set the console to shorter default clip lengths if you capture a lot. A storage warning in the middle of a once-only gameplay moment is a self-inflicted wound.

Installing the NVMe: the two details that matter

The install itself is a ten-minute job — console off and unplugged, cover off, one screw for the expansion slot, seat the drive, replace the screw and cover. The two details people miss:

  • The heatsink is not optional. PS5-spec drives either ship with one fitted or need a slim aftermarket heatsink that still fits under the slot cover. A bare drive will throttle and can fail early.
  • Use the spacer and screw in the slot matched to the 2280 length, and don't force the cover — if it won't sit flat, the heatsink is too tall.

The rule that ends the problem

Round one was about space. Round two is about habit: after every install spree, spend two minutes moving the finished games to cold storage. Storage you manage in small passes never becomes the Christmas-night crisis of deleting a game somebody in the house is still playing.

The games are the fun part. The drive is just the shelf — build a shelf big enough for the season.
