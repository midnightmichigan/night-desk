Learjet goes down in a Palm Beach County neighborhood — all 9 aboard killed

A private business jet fell out of the sky over Palm Beach County on Sunday afternoon and erupted in flames beside a busy roadway — and all nine people aboard died, U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

The aircraft, a Learjet 60 registered N722BD, crashed at about 12:50 p.m. on November 1 near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in the Greenacres area, close to the entrance of a mobile home community. The Federal Aviation Administration said the jet had departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was headed to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas.

As of Sunday afternoon, officials had received no reports of anyone on the ground killed or injured.

"We're gonna crash — we're out of time"

Air traffic recordings captured by LiveATC.net and reported by the Associated Press document the crew's final minutes. The pilots reported moderate turbulence, ice accumulation and precipitation at about 35,000 feet and asked for a better ride. After telling controllers they would climb, a mayday call came in.

The descent built from about 10,000 feet per minute to 20,000, and flight data reviewed by Flightradar24 showed the vertical rate eventually exceeding 32,000 feet per minute. In transmissions described by the AP, the pilot warned, "We're going to crash into the ground." The Palm Beach Post, citing the recordings, quoted the pilot: "We are descending 22,000 ft, and we are banking 60 degrees. We're out of time."

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis, at the scene, called it "a cataclysmic plane crash. It looked like a missile going down and left a big crater on the side of the road."

What investigators will examine

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, with the FAA sending a team to support a go-team on scene. Investigators will examine flight data, weather, and aircraft systems. A stalled frontal boundary was parked over the area Sunday, pushing freezing cloud tops tens of thousands of feet high — conditions that can cause icing trouble, as USA Today-Florida reported.

No cause has been determined. What the crew reported on the radio — turbulence and ice — describes what they were dealing with in the cockpit; it is not a finding about why the jet was lost.

Still developing

The Night Desk will update this article as the NTSB releases the victims' identities, the preliminary timeline, and any early findings.

Facts in this article are drawn from the Palm Beach Post's November 1 report (Kim Miller, USA Today-Florida), Associated Press reporting on the LiveATC recordings and Secretary Duffy's statement, and Flightradar24's flight-data summary. The cause of the crash has not been determined.
