More than 300 Italian schools evacuated after emailed bomb threat; officials say no concrete danger found

Schools across Italy emptied out on Wednesday, Nov. 11, after a single emailed bomb threat landed in inboxes across the country's state school system.

Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara said 312 schools had been evacuated after receiving the email threats, and described the alerts as presumed hoaxes. The Education Ministry said the email reached about 6,300 of Italy's roughly 7,500 state schools — far more than were evacuated — and no explosive device had been reported found.

The government's response

Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni convened senior ministers and intelligence officials and said there was no evidence of a concrete danger. Defense Minister Guido Crosetto said the emailed threats appeared aimed at sowing terror and concern.

Reuters reported that it saw a copy of the email, signed "Mikhail Andropov, leader of the Vilayat of Misoginistan" — the same signature reported on recent threats to Irish and Bulgarian newsrooms and on hoax bomb alerts against European flights. No source has verified that name or any such group, and no link between the cases has been established.

Beyond Italy

Similar messages reached schools in Scotland, Northern Ireland, and the Republic of Ireland. Police Scotland said it was treating the messages as hoaxes that did not represent a credible threat, and police in Northern Ireland said they were investigating menacing emails sent to more than 300 schools from a source outside the United Kingdom.
