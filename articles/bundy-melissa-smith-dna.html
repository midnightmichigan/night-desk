52 years later, DNA ties Ted Bundy to the murder of Melissa Ann Smith

A hunter found her in the woods in October 1974. This week — exactly 52 years later — Utah investigators finally put a name on who put her there.

New DNA testing has linked serial killer Ted Bundy to the murder of Melissa Ann Smith, 17, authorities announced at a news conference on Tuesday. Summit County Attorney Margaret Olson said investigators are satisfied Bundy was responsible for killing Smith, a death that had long been listed as a cold case.

How the case broke

Investigators closed the case by making crucial connections with DNA found on Smith and on Laura Aime, another Bundy victim whose case was resolved last April in Utah County. Bundy's DNA was already on file in Florida, where he was convicted and ultimately executed in 1989.

Beyond the DNA, Olson said, Smith's death showed hallmarks of Bundy's "pattern, motive, means and opportunity."

The technology mattered as much as the evidence. Utah's state crime lab received new equipment in 2023 that can extract DNA from samples even when they are small, degraded by age, or contain DNA from multiple people.

"This case demonstrates the importance of preserving evidence," said Salt Lake County Sheriff Rosie Rivera, whose department has also investigated homicides linked to Bundy. "Nobody is just going to walk away and just blame everything on Ted Bundy, even though many cases have been that way. Here in the state of Utah, we take it very seriously."

Who Melissa Smith was

Smith's father was the police chief in Midvale, south of Salt Lake City. She had been at a pizza parlor and a restaurant before she disappeared on October 18, 1974, The Salt Lake Tribune reported at the time. Nine days later, her body was found in Summit County.

Her parents are deceased. Her relatives were contacted about the findings but declined to attend the news conference and declined to speak to reporters, the Summit County Sheriff's Office said.

The book is not closed

Bundy was executed by electric chair in Florida in 1989 after being convicted of three murders, including the killing of two women at the Chi Omega sorority house at Florida State University. He killed at least 30 women and girls in multiple states in the 1970s, and in his final days gave confessions to crimes that had not previously been confirmed — not all of them have been.

Utah investigators say the work continues. Besides Florida and Utah, Bundy had victims in Colorado, Idaho and Washington state, and authorities are especially working to solve the 1974 death of 16-year-old Nancy Wilcox of Utah.

"With every case closure, may we stomp the memory of Ted Bundy out," said Utah County Sgt. Mike Reynolds.

Solving the Smith homicide "does not close the book on Ted Bundy," said Det. Ben Pender of the Salt Lake County Sheriff's Office, which holds the Wilcox investigation. "Information kind of leads to Ted Bundy but again we're not for certain on Nancy Wilcox. We're going to continue to work that case."
