Virginia State Sen. L. Louise Lucas Indicted on Federal Racketeering, Marijuana Charges

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk

Federal prosecutors announced Thursday that Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, 82, has been indicted on federal racketeering, marijuana-distribution, money-laundering, and bribery charges — one of the highest-profile corruption cases in Virginia's modern political history.

What the indictment says

According to a 45-page indictment filed Oct. 14 in the Eastern District of Virginia, Lucas and six co-defendants conspired to distribute and possess marijuana, conspired to launder money, laundered money, and committed bribery by distributing large quantities of illegal marijuana under the auspices of her legal medical cannabis business, The Cannabis Outlet, in Portsmouth, Virginia.

The co-defendants are Lisa Lucas Burke, Theresa Lucas-Lamb (Lucas's two daughters), Ryan Eichelberger (her grandson), Prince Albert Williamson Hudson, Carlton Upton, Jr., and Tameka Johnson.

Prosecutors allege Lucas arranged the sourcing and distribution of the marijuana, supervised the laundering of the proceeds, used her political influence to demand and accept a bribe, and tried to influence Virginia legislation to benefit herself and her family.

"The charges listed in the indictment reflect the alleged behavior of a public servant who put her own financial interests before the people she was elected to serve," U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said at the press conference.

Surrender and press conference

Lucas surrendered to authorities Thursday morning, and all seven defendants were in federal custody, Blanche said. Initial appearances were expected Thursday afternoon in Norfolk federal court.

The FBI searched Lucas's Portsmouth office and The Cannabis Outlet on May 6 as part of the investigation.

The political fight

Lucas is among Virginia's most prominent Democrats — Senate president pro tempore, a state senator since 1992, an outspoken critic of President Trump, and a key leader of the state's recent redistricting effort. In a statement posted on X after the indictment was announced, she wrote that she would "fight back":

"I grew up under Jim Crow. I've seen what happens when people in power try to silence those who stand up against them. I've spent my life fighting back, and I'm not stopping now. I will not be intimidated. I will not be bullied, and I will not back down."

Blanche rejected suggestions of political motivation, saying the investigation "began many years ago" and "wasn't started... by the Trump administration." FBI Director Kash Patel, who also spoke at the press conference, accused Lucas of using "her position of power and public trust in the Virginia state senate to deflect and defend against her own criminal enterprise."
