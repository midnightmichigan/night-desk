'American Idol' alum Caleb Flynn convicted of murdering wife Ashley; sentencing set for Monday

By The Night Desk

Booking photo: Tipp City Police Department / via Extra; courtroom photo: The Tab; graphic: Dayton 24/7 Now / WKEF

A former "American Idol" contestant and Ohio worship pastor has been convicted of murdering his wife in her sleep — and on Monday afternoon, a judge will decide how the rest of his life goes.

Caleb Flynn, 40, was found guilty Tuesday, September 29, on all nine counts in the shooting death of his wife, Ashley Flynn, at their Tipp City, Ohio home on February 16, 2026. The charges: aggravated murder, three counts of murder, two counts of felonious assault, and three counts of tampering with evidence. He was also convicted of firearm specifications on seven of the counts, which add prison time.

The jury needed less than three hours to reach the verdict, according to WKEF and NewsNation. Ashley's family embraced one another in the courtroom when the verdict was read, per the Dayton Daily News.

The 911 call that started it

On the morning of February 16, Flynn called 911 and told the dispatcher that an intruder had broken into the house and shot his wife. Officers found Ashley Flynn, 37 — a substitute teacher and middle-school volleyball coach — dead in bed from two gunshot wounds to the back of the head. The couple's two young daughters were home when police arrived.

Flynn was arrested days later. Prosecutors said there was no intruder: he shot Ashley himself, then staged the house to look like a break-in.

How the staged break-in fell apart

An FBI agent testified at trial that she found no pry, kick, or ram marks on the door Flynn claimed had been forced open. Instead, there were gouge marks on the inside of the frame — not consistent with a forced entry from the outside.

Prosecutors also told the jury that Flynn's fingerprints and DNA were found throughout the scene, and data from his devices showed him moving around the house during a period when he had told investigators he was asleep in another room. The gun used to kill Ashley has never been recovered.

The affair and the texts

Central to the prosecution's case: Flynn's nearly 18-month affair with Alleigha Botner, whom he met when she was a church intern. Prosecutors built their case around more than 107,000 text messages exchanged between the two.

In one message from February 2025 — almost exactly a year before the killing — Flynn wrote: "I need her to die, to cheat again, to leave." In August 2025, he texted Botner: "I was up until 4 a.m. last night crying, literally thinking of ways to kill her and not go to jail." The night before the shooting, he messaged her: "I choose you. I'm free. Actions will come tomorrow."

Closing arguments painted a motive of control: prosecutor Matthew Joseph said Flynn wanted to leave his marriage but feared losing custody of his daughters, his job at his wife's family business, and his position at the church.

The defense called no witnesses and Flynn did not testify. His attorney, Patrick Mulligan, argued the prosecution's case rested on "guesses, assumptions, and speculations" — and after the verdict said the defense will appeal, citing "a lack of evidence on prior calculation and design."

What happens Monday

Sentencing is scheduled for 3 p.m. Monday, October 5, before Miami County Common Pleas Judge Jeannine Pratt. Flynn faces anywhere from 20 years to life in prison — up to life without parole. The death penalty is off the table: Ohio requires specific death-penalty specifications for capital punishment, and prosecutors never charged them.

Flynn's $2 million bond was revoked after the verdict. He is being held at the Miami County Jail.
