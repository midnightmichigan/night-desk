40 Years Later: Guilty Verdict in the Christmas Eve 1985 Killing of Leslie McCray

Nearly 41 years after 17-year-old Leslie McCray was abducted from her Jacksonville home on Christmas Eve, a judge found the man who took her guilty of her murder.

The verdict

On Thursday, October 22, 2026, Duval County Judge Meredith Charbula convicted David Nelson Austin, 65, of first-degree murder, two counts of armed kidnapping and armed sexual battery in the 1985 rape and killing of McCray, a University of North Florida student.

Austin waived his right to a jury trial and left the verdict to the judge. He did not testify, and his defense called no witnesses. Sentencing is scheduled for a later date. Austin is already serving two life sentences in Michigan for separate sexual battery crimes committed in 1988.

McCray's family was in the courtroom as the verdict was read. Her cousin Joey Bray said the family had waited decades for accountability — "They took her from us and just left an empty hole that was never filled."

Christmas Eve, 1985

Shortly after 1 a.m. on December 24, 1985, an intruder broke into the duplex McCray shared with her roommate, Edgar "Wood" McCurry, then 20. The intruder tied McCurry up and took McCray. McCurry later testified he heard her plead with the man not to cut her. He managed to free himself and called police around 6 a.m.

McCray's body was found that morning in a ditch off Old Middleburg Road, a few miles from her home. She had been raped, beaten and stabbed.

A case that would not stay buried

Investigators suspended the case in 1986. In 2019, McCray's family asked the Jacksonville Sheriff's Office Cold Case Unit — and the Jacksonville-based nonprofit Project: Cold Case — to take another look at the evidence.

In 2020, investigators resubmitted evidence for DNA testing with newer technology. The resulting profile matched Austin in a Michigan corrections database. Detectives interviewed him in Michigan in April 2021, and further testing using a sample taken directly from him confirmed the match. He was arrested in the case that year.

For years, McCurry — the surviving roommate — had been a focus of suspicion. When detectives revisited the case, his DNA did not match the evidence from the 1985 sexual-assault kit, clearing him. McCurry testified he did not know or recognize Austin.

Assistant State Attorney Lauren Anderson credited the family's persistence — and urged anyone with unresolved cases in their family to press investigators to review evidence that may be suitable for new testing.

What came next

Cold case detective Harmony Brooks drew specific praise from the family for pushing the case back to life. Project: Cold Case's role in keeping the evidence — and the family's hope — intact is the quiet engine behind this conviction: without the 2020 resubmission, there would have been no match, no arrest, and no verdict.

Charges and convictions referenced here are matters of public record. This article is based on reporting by The Florida Times-Union (USA Today Network) and the Jacksonville Sheriff's Office.
