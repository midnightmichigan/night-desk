Russell Brand sentenced to 10½ years for rape and sexual assaults

It's over. Russell Brand is going to prison for a decade.

The comedian and actor, 51, was sentenced Monday at Southwark Crown Court in London to 10 years and six months in prison, after a jury last week convicted him of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women.

The sentence

Mr Justice Joel Bennathan imposed nine years for the rape, reduced from ten years for mitigating factors. For the three sexual assaults he imposed nine months, 18 months (reduced from two years), and eight months, to run concurrently with each other but consecutively to the rape sentence, bringing the total to 10 years and six months.

Brand must serve two-thirds of the sentence in custody before he is eligible for release on license. He had faced a maximum of life in prison.

In his sentencing remarks, the judge said Brand had become a "sexual predator" during the height of his fame and that the offences were fueled by his "staggering arrogance." He also accepted, given how long ago the offences were committed and evidence from Brand's wife, that there was no sound evidential basis to conclude Brand poses a future risk.

The case

The convictions cover offences against four women between 2004 and 2009. Brand was acquitted of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. The trial, which began Oct. 12 and ran about four weeks, featured testimony from six accusers. Brand denied the charges and maintained his sexual encounters were consensual.

He was remanded at HMP Wandsworth after Thursday's verdict and was brought back to court Monday, where victim impact statements were heard before the sentence was passed.

Source: Associated Press and wire reporting from Southwark Crown Court, London.
