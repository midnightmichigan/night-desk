Nearly 40 years later: arrest in the 1986 Spokane cold-case murder of Mary Ann Turner

Nearly 40 years later: arrest in the 1986 Spokane cold-case murder of Mary Ann Turner

She fought her killer — and nearly forty years later, the evidence of that fight helped name him.

On October 21, 2026, Spokane police arrested David Tibbett, 74, at his home on East Decatur and charged him with second-degree murder in the 1986 killing of Mary Ann Turner, 32. It is the same house Tibbett lived in when police say Turner was killed.

The night in 1986

On the morning of November 4, 1986, police found Turner dead in an alley near South Ivory Street and East Pacific Avenue in east Spokane. She was partially clothed, wearing a scarf that may have been used to strangle her, with one earring found in the dirt nearby. An autopsy determined she had been strangled to death.

Investigators wrote in a search warrant that crime-scene and autopsy photographs showed Turner fought her killer violently — her hands were heavily soiled and her fingernails clogged with dirt. Inside those fingernails was a clue that would outlast every lead of the next four decades: DNA.

A fellow woman working the streets with Turner told police she watched Turner get picked up by a burnt-orange two-door car early that morning. She never saw Turner again. About a week later, she picked a 1977 Ford Thunderbird from a photo book as resembling the car, and described the driver as a white man in his 40s with a "mushroom" haircut and glasses.

Tips came in — one caller to the Secret Witness tipline named David Tibbett, saying he had a copper-brown Ford Elite and was "known to hang around the hookers." Detectives found Tibbett was already in jail on an eluding charge from after Turner's murder. He refused to speak with police, but investigators determined he had a 1975 Ford Elite registered to his East Decatur home. Then the case went cold.

The DNA journey

Evidence preserved from the scene included four cigarette butts, fingernail clippings and swabs. In 1987, the Washington State Crime Lab found microscopic amounts of blood in the fingernails — too little to test with the technology of the time. In 2008, a private lab found semen from several swabs, a partial DNA profile from a cigarette butt, and a mixed DNA profile from the fingernail clippings: Turner's, plus an unknown male. That profile went into a national database. Even as recently as 2025, there was no match.

Then technology caught up. In 2025, Spokane police sent the DNA to Othram, a Texas lab specializing in forensic-grade genome sequencing and forensic genetic genealogy. By January 2026, Othram told police the profile was linked to three brothers: Darrel Tibbett, Daniel Tibbett and David Tibbett. Darrel and Daniel both died in 2013. David Tibbett was still in Spokane — still living in the house on East Decatur.

Court records also show Tibbett was arrested in Spokane in 1968 for sexual assault and again for attempted rape in 1971, and was designated a sexual psychopath during an evaluation at Western State Hospital.

The ruse

In November 2025, Tibbett reported his car stolen; the vehicle was found by U.S. Border Patrol and returned to him. In February 2026, police decided to "employ a ruse": they mailed him a letter about the stolen car, asking him to confirm it was no longer stolen by signing the letter and mailing it back.

According to the search warrant, Tibbett signed the letter, licked the envelope and sent it back. Police took the envelope, unopened, to the Washington State Crime Lab and asked for a comparison against the DNA found under Turner's fingernails. On March 11, the news came back: it was a match.

On October 21, police obtained a search warrant for Tibbett's home, searched it, and arrested him. He was booked into the Spokane County Jail on a charge of second-degree murder. The investigation remains ongoing.

David Tibbett is accused, not convicted. He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

Nearly forty years after Mary Ann Turner was found in that alley, the man accused of killing her will face charges for the crime. The Night Desk will follow this one — the court proceedings, and the science that finally closed the gap.
