VERDICT: Guilty on all counts — the 14-year Faith Hedgepeth cold case finally gets its answer

Late Friday afternoon, after more than fourteen years of waiting, the Faith Hedgepeth case got its verdict. A Durham County jury found Miguel Salguero-Olivares, 33, guilty of first-degree murder, first-degree rape, first-degree sexual offense, and first-degree burglary in the 2012 killing of 19-year-old University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill student Faith Hedgepeth.

The night that started it all

On September 7, 2012, Hedgepeth was found beaten to death in the off-campus apartment she shared with her friend Karena Rosario. The case went cold for nearly a decade — until investigators matched DNA evidence from the crime scene to Salguero-Olivares. He was arrested in September 2021, nine years after her death, and pleaded not guilty in November 2024.

The trial

Friday was the 19th day of the trial, counting jury selection. Testimony began October 5, and in a move that stunned the courtroom, the defense called no witnesses — resting its case the moment the prosecution finished.

The jury began deliberating Thursday afternoon and asked to see summaries of lab reports and fingerprint readings. By late Friday, they had convicted on everything.

Prosecutors argued Salguero-Olivares entered the apartment after Rosario left early that morning, sexually assaulted Hedgepeth, and beat her to death with a glass rum bottle — revenge, they said, after she rebuffed his advance and embarrassed him in front of his friends. The defense challenged what the DNA could prove about timing, questioned the investigation, and pointed to unanswered questions — including how he found the apartment and the meaning of a handwritten message left on a paper bag on her bed.

The sentence

Judge Keith Gregory sentenced Salguero-Olivares immediately after the verdict: life in prison without the possibility of parole for the murder conviction — the mandatory sentence, since the district attorney did not seek the death penalty. The remaining terms — 64 to 89 months for burglary, 240 to 348 months each for rape and sexual offense — run consecutively. The defense says it will appeal. As the verdict was read, Salguero-Olivares protested: "It's not fair."

The family

Roland Hedgepeth told the court exactly how long they'd waited: 14 years, six weeks and four days. 169 months and 18 days. 5,158 days. His wife Connie said the family finally has the closure and peace they needed — and that she has forgiven. After the proceedings ended, Judge Gregory hugged both parents.

A case that haunted Chapel Hill for fourteen years finally has its answer. The Night Desk will keep digging on this one — the DNA journey, the questions that still linger, and what comes next on appeal.
