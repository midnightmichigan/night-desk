New witness leads to arrest in 2013 Phoenix cold case killing of security guard Jeffrey Bellemare

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk

New witness leads to arrest in 2013 Phoenix cold case killing of security guard Jeffrey Bellemare

Information from a new witness who came forward more than a dozen years after security guard Jeffrey Bellemare was killed on the job led to the recent arrest of a suspect in the murder, Phoenix police records say.

Joe Alexander Gerardo, 43, was arrested November 3 in Globe and refused to give any statements to law enforcement, according to a probable cause statement in his arrest record. He is in a Maricopa County jail on a $2 million bond and was booked on one count of first-degree murder. Information about his attorney was not available November 10.

The killing — November 29, 2013

The Phoenix Police Department has said two people confronted 59-year-old Jeffrey Bellemare in a dark portion of an apartment complex at 3810 N. Maryvale Parkway and shot him twice with a .40-caliber weapon before hopping in a car and driving away. The car was described by witnesses as an older-model sedan with a vinyl top and possible front-end damage.

Bellemare, who was unarmed and wearing a uniform, was pronounced dead within 15 minutes. Multiple witnesses told police a man and a woman ran from the scene, and that a male by the name of "Tweety" was responsible for the shooting — but the information they gave was, until 2026, never enough.

Bellemare had spent November 28, 2013 with his family. He put on his uniform and went to work the overnight shift that began on Thanksgiving Day. He was shot and killed at about 1:30 a.m. on November 29, 2013, police said. He was a retired commercial carpenter living in Surprise who had taken security work to stay busy and supplement his fixed retirement income.

The new witness

In early 2026, a new female witness came forward who said she was a teenager in 2013 and had seen a male and a female at an apartment in the complex minutes before the shooting, the probable cause statement says.

She told police the male suspect targeted Bellemare and said he was "going to handle him tonight." She later heard two gunshots and, heading back to her apartment, saw the male and female running away from Bellemare's body.

The witness identified Gerardo in a photographic lineup, according to the probable cause statement. Police say they then discovered Gerardo had connections to another occupant who had lived in the complex, was known to carry a .40-caliber handgun around the time of the murder, owned and operated a 1993 Chrysler Imperial sedan with front-end damage, and was a documented gang member with the moniker Tweety.

Booking information says Gerardo has prior convictions for armed robbery and aggravated assault. At his initial appearance, prosecutor Dawn Sauer said the state believes Gerardo is "extremely violent."

"There is no such thing as a cold case"

At a news conference about the arrest outside Phoenix police headquarters on November 10, Sgt. John Buchanan told reporters that for police "there is no such thing as a cold case," that "any unsolved homicide is always being worked," and that witnesses should always come forward, even if days, months or years have passed.

The Bellemare family told The Arizona Republic on November 9 the arrest is a relief but sad at the same time, especially so close to Thanksgiving and the 13th anniversary of Bellemare's murder.

"It's always been a hard time of year for us. ... You never get over it," Karan Bellemare told The Republic. "All of us feel bittersweet. We miss him. He's missing out on things he should be here for."
