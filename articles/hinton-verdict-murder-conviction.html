VERDICT: Rodney Hinton Jr. convicted of murder in the deputy-killing crash that shook Cincinnati

The jury has spoken. Rodney Hinton Jr. was convicted of murder Friday in the May 2025 crash that killed Hamilton County Sheriff's Deputy Larry Ray Henderson Jr. — a case The Night Desk has followed since jury selection began earlier this month.

The verdict

Jurors also found Hinton guilty of reckless homicide and felonious assault. Henderson, a deputy who had spent decades with the Hamilton County Sheriff's Office, had retired from the department a few months before the crash but was still working off-duty assignments. He was directing traffic for a college graduation when Hinton's car struck him.

Hinton faces 15 years to life in prison on the murder conviction, with the possibility of parole. Sentencing is scheduled for November 16. His attorney, Clyde Bennett II, said he will appeal.

The case that split a city

The crash came just hours after Hinton and other family members watched police body-camera footage showing a Cincinnati police officer fatally shoot his 18-year-old son, Ryan Hinton. The teenager was shot twice during a chase after officers said he pointed a gun at them; the officer was later found to be legally justified in his use of force and was not charged.

Prosecutors acknowledged Hinton was distraught and grieving — but argued he made a choice to aim his car at the deputy, and that his mental illnesses didn't stop him from understanding right from wrong.

The defense's argument

Hinton pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity. Bennett argued in closing that his client was trying to kill himself when he drove into the intersection, describing a psychotic episode. Hinton was diagnosed with schizoaffective disorder and other mental illnesses. The jury rejected the insanity defense after deliberating.

Hamilton County Sheriff Charmaine McGuffey said the verdict brought some closure and helped provide justice for Henderson, who was remembered at his funeral as a selfless colleague and dedicated public servant who regularly volunteered for the most dangerous assignments.

What happens next

  • November 16: sentencing
  • The defense says it will appeal the conviction
  • The Night Desk will keep following this case through sentencing

Deputy Henderson's death is a reminder that behind every headline is a family. Our thoughts are with the Hendersons — and with every law-enforcement family watching this case.
