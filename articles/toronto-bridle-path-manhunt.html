Canada-wide manhunt after Toronto mansion home invasion

A family asleep in one of Toronto's wealthiest neighbourhoods. Three masked intruders upstairs with guns and a hammer. And a suspect who, police say, was shot by one of his own accomplices — then walked out of a hospital before anyone knew to hold him.

That is the case Toronto police laid out on Saturday, October 31, when they identified Daijohn Richards, 23, of Toronto as one of three suspects in the violent home invasion at the Bridle Path home of MAC Group CEO David Morton.

What police say happened

At about 3:45 a.m. on Thursday, October 29, 2026, three masked intruders in hoodies and ski masks entered the six-bedroom home and confronted Morton, 75, his wife Doriana Zohil-Morton, 64, and their adult daughters Maggie and Cristina upstairs, demanding valuables. An altercation broke out.

Morton and both daughters were shot. His wife was attacked with a hammer. Inspector Kristy Smith said two of the victims suffered "life-altering injuries" and remained hospitalized, while the others were treated for non-life-threatening wounds.

All three suspects escaped and remained at large late Saturday.

The hospital twist

Investigators believe Richards was accidentally shot in the right shoulder by one of the other two robbers during the struggle. He went to St. Joseph's Health Centre for treatment, and staff reported the gunshot wound to police under Ontario's mandatory reporting rules.

He was discharged late Thursday morning — before investigators had established grounds linking him to the home invasion. Smith told reporters police did not arrest him at the hospital because there was not yet enough evidence connecting him to the crime. Forensic work and neighbourhood canvassing later established that connection, police said.

Richards may be showing signs of the shoulder injury, police said. All three suspects are considered armed and dangerous — do not approach them.

The charges

Richards is wanted on a Canada-wide warrant. Toronto Police say the charges include:

  • Four counts of discharging a firearm with intent to wound
  • Four counts of robbery with a firearm
  • Three counts of aggravated assault
  • Break and enter, possession of a prohibited device, and disguise with intent

How to help

Anyone with information, or who knows where Richards is, can contact the Toronto Police Hold Up Squad at 416-808-7350 or 33 Division at 416-808-3300.

In a statement, Morton said: "It is difficult to put into words the trauma of experiencing something so horrific in the one place where everyone should feel safest: their own home. No family should ever have to experience this."

Charges are allegations. Richards is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in a court of law.
