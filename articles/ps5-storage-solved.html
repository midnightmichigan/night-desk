PS5 Storage, Solved: How to Choose an External Drive for a Growing Game Library

PS5 Storage, Solved: How to Choose an External Drive for a Growing Game Library

A PS5 fills up fast. A few big games and the internal drive is suddenly a negotiation every time something new comes home. There are two different storage problems, and they have two different answers — mixing them up is how people buy the wrong drive.

Problem 1: "I need somewhere to put all these games" — an external USB drive

An external USB hard drive plugs into the PS5 and stores your whole library. One honest limitation to know before you buy: PS5 games stored on a USB drive can't be played straight from it — you copy them back to the internal drive to play (still much faster than re-downloading). PS4 games, on the other hand, can play directly from USB storage.

What to look for:

  • Size: 2TB is the floor we'd bother with; 4TB–5TB is the sweet spot for a family library that keeps growing.
  • Form factor: a slim portable drive (bus-powered, no wall plug) is the tidy choice next to a console.
  • Speed: for storage-and-copy duty, a standard portable HDD is fine. You don't need SSD speed for a game warehouse.

→ Portable external hard drives, 4TB–5TB

Problem 2: "I want more games playable at once" — an internal NVMe SSD

The PS5 has an internal expansion slot for an M.2 NVMe SSD, and games installed there play exactly like the built-in storage. This is the upgrade that actually expands your playable library.

What to look for:

  • PCIe Gen4 — Gen3 drives don't meet the PS5's requirement.
  • Read speed 5,500 MB/s or faster (Sony's stated recommendation).
  • A heatsink — required. Buy one with a heatsink fitted, or a low-profile heatsink that fits the slot's cover; the slot is not tall.
  • Size: 1TB doubles most people's usable space; 2TB ends the storage conversation for years.

→ Gen4 NVMe SSDs with heatsink for PS5

Which one should you buy first?

  • Mostly PS4 games and a big backlog you rotate through? External drive first — cheapest gigabytes you'll ever buy.
  • Mostly PS5 games and you're tired of deleting? NVMe first — it's the only one that adds playable space.
  • A household where new games arrive constantly? Eventually both: NVMe for what's in rotation, external for the archive.

Either way, buy the size that feels slightly too big. With game libraries, it never is for long.
