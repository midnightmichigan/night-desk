Nine Months On: Sheriff Says DNA Work Continues in the Nancy Guthrie Case

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk

Nine months after Nancy Guthrie vanished from her Tucson home, investigators have issued a rare public update — and it centers on DNA.

On Friday, October 30, the Pima County Sheriff's Department shared a new statement from Sheriff Chris Nanos via X.

"The Nancy Guthrie Task Force continues to work with our labs in hopes of furthering our DNA analysis in this investigation," the statement began. "Earlier this month, investigators traveled to Florida to meet with lab scientists to see what more could be done to advance the case. A number of ideas were discussed, and we continue to work with all of our partners to solve this case."

"This case isn't cold"

The statement echoed remarks Nanos made in a recent radio interview on KVOI AM 1030's Bill Buckmaster Show.

"This case isn't cold because we still have evidence that we're working with," Nanos said. "Just last week, we sent a team of investigators to our labs in Florida to meet with those lab techs."

He said labs in Kansas, San Francisco, Washington, and Phoenix are also contributing, and that investigators are "constantly talking about what we can do with the evidence we have, and how we can get to a point where we feel comfortable that we can make an identification."

Where the case stands

  • Nancy Guthrie, 84, mother of Today co-anchor Savannah Guthrie, was last seen at her Catalina Foothills home on the evening of January 31; her family reported her missing on February 1.
  • Investigators have released photos of a masked suspect on her front porch and published alleged ransom notes.
  • No suspect has been named and no arrest has been announced.
  • Savannah Guthrie has offered a $1 million reward for information leading to an arrest.

The ask

"We want to remind the public that no tip is too small," Nanos wrote. "Even the smallest piece of information could be critical to this investigation. If you know something, please call 88-CRIME or 1-800-CALL-FBI."

If you have information about Nancy Guthrie's disappearance, call 88-CRIME or the FBI at 1-800-CALL-FBI.
