Water Softener Salt: The Upkeep Schedule Nobody Tells You

Our first softener-salt guide covered choosing salt. This one covers the part that actually determines whether your softener works in year three: the upkeep rhythm. A water softener is a forgiving appliance right up until it isn't — it fails silently, and you find out from spotty dishes, itchy laundry, and scale creeping back onto fixtures weeks after the brine tank quietly stopped doing its job. December adds a twist: holiday guests mean showers, laundry, and dishes at double rate, and a softener drinks salt in proportion. Here is the schedule.

Monthly: the two-minute check

Once a month, lift the brine tank lid and look. That's most of the job.

  • Salt level: keep the tank between one-third and two-thirds full, and always above the water line in the brine well. Running a tank to empty doesn't just stop softening — it lets the resin bed foul, and that's the expensive version of the lesson.
  • Salt bridging: tap the salt crust with a broom handle. If it sounds hollow, a hard crust ("bridge") has formed over a void — the salt looks full but nothing is feeding the brine. Break it gently and let the salt settle.
  • Mush and sludge: a layer of mushy salt at the bottom is normal in small amounts; a tank that's mostly mush needs a clean-out (below).

Which salt, revisited — for upkeep, not marketing

  • Evaporated salt pellets are the cleanest option — highest purity, least residue in the tank, fewest clean-outs. The upkeep-friendly default.
  • Solar salt (crystals or pellets) costs less and works fine, with slightly more insoluble residue — budget one extra tank inspection a year.
  • Rock salt is the false economy: the most sediment, the most mushing, the most clean-outs. Skip it.
  • Potassium chloride is the alternative where sodium is a concern (septic considerations, low-sodium households, plants) — it works in the same tank, costs more, and may need the softener's salt setting adjusted.

Stocking for the season

Salt is heavy, cheap, and miserable to emergency-buy in a snowstorm. The household rule: never let the last bag be the one in the tank. Keep at least one spare bag stored dry (off a concrete floor, on a board or pallet corner) at all times, and two going into the holidays — guest usage is real, and a 40-pound bag disappears into December laundry faster than the calendar suggests. Buy bags on ordinary grocery or hardware runs, not as a special trip.

Once a year: the brine tank clean-out

Pick a mild month. Let the salt run low, scoop out what remains, and scrub the tank with dish soap and water; check the brine well and float assembly while you're in there. Refill with fresh salt. An hour, once a year, and it's the difference between a softener that lasts a decade and one that clogs itself into an early replacement. While you're at it, confirm the regeneration schedule on the control head still matches your household size — a softener set for two people, serving a growing family plus holiday guests, regenerates too rarely and feels broken when it isn't.

The symptom cheat-sheet

  • Spots and scale returning gradually: salt is empty, bridged, or the tank needs cleaning — check salt first.
  • Soft water right after regeneration, hard by mid-week: regeneration frequency or hardness setting is too low for current usage.
  • Salty-tasting water: a rinse-cycle or drain issue — check the drain line for kinks before calling anyone.
  • No change after topping up salt: the bridge, not the level, was the problem all along.

A softener asks for two minutes a month and an hour a year. Pay it, and the appliance disappears into the background — which, heading into the busiest water month of the year, is exactly where it belongs.
