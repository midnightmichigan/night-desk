Spirit Halloween shooting — teen publicly named, charged with two murders; prosecutors seek adult trial

The 17-year-old arrested in the Spirit Halloween store shooting in Fairfield, California has been publicly named by prosecutors and charged with two counts of murder, and the Solano County District Attorney's Office is asking a judge to try him as an adult.

The charges

Monte Smith, 17, was arrested Monday, Nov. 2 at a residence in neighboring Suisun City in the Oct. 30 shooting at the Spirit Halloween store inside Solano Town Center, Fairfield police said. Police initially withheld his name because of his age; the DA's office later publicly identified him in its charging announcement.

The DA's office charged Smith with:

  • Two counts of murder, with a multiple-murder special circumstance allegation
  • Three counts of attempted murder
  • Three counts of assault with a semiautomatic weapon
  • Multiple firearm enhancements
  • An additional allegation tied to a prior violent-offense conviction classified as a strike (the DA did not specify the underlying offense)

Prosecutors have filed a petition to transfer Smith from juvenile court to adult criminal court, a move a judge must approve, and say they will argue he should remain detained: "Due to the seriousness of these crimes, the Minor's criminal record, and the threat to public safety, the People will argue that Smith remain detained," the DA's office said.

Nov. 4 hearing: held without bail, arraignment postponed

Smith made his first court appearance on Wednesday, Nov. 4. Prosecutors described the attack as an act of terrorism, saying Smith allegedly followed a man he had a previous issue with into the crowded store and opened fire as families waited in the checkout line. They also disclosed that Hages was 13 weeks pregnant; the second murder charge is tied to the death of her unborn child. Four victims remained hospitalized Wednesday; two had been released. Investigators have collected more than 90 witness statements.

Smith's alternate public defender asked that he be released to a home placement. The judge rejected that request and ordered Smith to remain in juvenile detention without bail. His arraignment was postponed to Nov. 13 at 9 a.m. in Solano County Juvenile Court to give his defense attorney more time to review police reports and surveillance footage. Chief Deputy District Attorney Bruce Flynn said the decision on whether Smith will be prosecuted as an adult could take several weeks; under California's Proposition 57, a judge must find clear and convincing evidence that a minor cannot be rehabilitated within the juvenile system before approving a transfer.

Smith is charged, not convicted, and is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court.

A prior shooting case

Fairfield police said Smith had already been arrested in another shooting before the Spirit Halloween attack — gunfire in a local high school parking lot after school hours in 2025. He received a sentence in that case, served time in juvenile hall, and was later released, the department said. Police have not identified the school or said when he was released.

Fairfield Police Chief Dan Marshall, speaking to KTVU, said: "It's time for change in California. I don't know how many more people have to die at the hands of youth. Hopefully we've had plenty enough."

The Oct. 30 shooting

The gunfire erupted around 8:30 p.m. on Oct. 30, after Smith allegedly walked into the crowded store, spotted a customer investigators believe was his intended target, and opened fire — striking that person and six others before fleeing within minutes, police said. The intended target has not been identified and no motive has been released.

Constance Hages, 31, died at the scene. Police confirmed Hages was pregnant; her death leaves four children without their mother, according to a GoFundMe organized by her aunt, Clanisha Morton.

In a Saturday police update on the surviving victims, a 16-year-old boy, a 19-year-old man and a 32-year-old man were in critical condition, a 23-year-old man was in critical but stable condition, and two other men, ages 19 and 43, had been treated and released.

A school resource officer recognized Smith in surveillance footage within hours of the shooting, which is how investigators identified him so quickly. The FBI's Evidence Response Team helped Fairfield police process part of the crime scene.

Anyone with information is asked to contact Fairfield police at 707-428-7600 or email 1030mall@fairfield.ca.gov.

The Night Desk first covered the arrest on Nov. 2 and will follow the Nov. 13 arraignment and the adult-transfer decision.
