Seven Killed in Attack on Church Funeral in Jordan; Manhunt Underway

Seven people were killed and 34 wounded on Friday, October 23, when gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hamoud, in Jordan's Karak governorate, according to Jordan's Public Security Directorate. It is a rare attack on the country's Christian minority, which authorities are calling an unprecedented terrorist act.

The attack

The shooting began shortly after 2:00 p.m. local time, while a funeral service was underway with between 150 and 200 people inside or just outside the church. Witnesses told Jordan's state-owned Al-Mamlaka television that masked gunmen fired for several minutes — first at people outside the building, then inside it — before fleeing.

Security camera footage aired by Al-Mamlaka and verified by Reuters showed three armed men approaching the church grounds on foot, firing at the building from a sidewalk while taking cover behind trees and a low wall, then running toward the church entrance. Reporting on the footage, Al Arabiya said one attacker carried an automatic weapon while the other two carried pistols. Attendees described hiding under pews, fleeing through doors, and finding bodies in the courtyard.

The gunmen then left the area in a car and remain at large. Funeral attendee Khaled Burqan told Al-Mamlaka that dozens of people were present and that the gunmen fired on them for several minutes.

Official response

Public Security Directorate chief Major General Abdallah al-Maaytah confirmed the toll at a press conference in Karak: seven dead, 34 injured, with most of the wounded still receiving treatment at hospitals including Prince Ali Military Hospital. An initial government figure of six dead was later revised to seven, and officials have said the toll could still rise.

Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya described the attackers as a group of extremists and called the assault an unprecedented terrorist act carried out by people with "no relationship to humanity." He said it was too early to tell whether the attackers had links to known groups inside or outside Jordan, and no group has claimed responsibility. King Abdullah II said Jordan "will confront the terrorist act that targeted Karak with the harshest penalties," offered condolences to the families, and visited a hospital treating the wounded. The government declared three days of mourning, with flags at half-staff.

Condemnation came from across the region and beyond: the U.S. Embassy in Amman issued condolences and a security advisory warning Americans to expect heightened police presence, while the Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem, Sunni Islam's Al-Azhar Institute, Iraq's government, Lebanon's president, and Syria's foreign ministry all denounced the attack.

Why it matters

Sectarian attacks are rare in Jordan, where Christians make up about 2% of the population. Karak, however, has a significant Christian community — and a recent history of violence: in December 2016, gunmen killed 10 people in Karak, including at the Crusader castle popular with tourists, in an attack claimed by the Islamic State group. No connection between the 2016 attack and Friday's shooting has been established.

This is a developing story. The casualty count and details of the investigation may change as Jordanian authorities release more information. This article is based on reporting by Reuters, CNN, and the Associated Press.
