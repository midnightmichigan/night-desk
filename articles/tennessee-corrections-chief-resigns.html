Tennessee's Prisons Chief Resigns After Failed Execution of Christa Pike

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk

Tennessee's top prison official is out.

Gov. Bill Lee announced Saturday that Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada has resigned and will leave his position this month — days after the state's failed attempt to execute Christa Pike by lethal injection left her gravely ill in a hospital.

"Commissioner Strada has served with integrity, and I appreciate his willingness to put the interests of Tennesseans first during this difficult moment," Lee said in a statement.

In a separate statement, Strada said that although he believes agency personnel followed proper protocol, "stepping aside is in the best interest of Tennesseans as the independent review moves forward."

What happened at Riverbend

On Wednesday night (Sept. 30), Tennessee attempted to execute Pike — the state's first female death-row inmate in over 200 years, sentenced to death in 1996 for her role in the torture and killing of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer in 1995 — at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville.

Pike, 50, survived two doses of the lethal drug pentobarbital. Her lawyers wrote in a Friday court filing that she was left unconscious, intubated and hooked to a mechanical ventilator to breathe, with hospital staff working to clear the drug from her system. Pike's attorneys believe problems with the IV lines caused the pentobarbital to flow into her tissue instead of her bloodstream:

"Apparently at no point did any member of the execution team realize that the IV lines were not correctly placed or that the veins had blown and that the pentobarbital was, in whole or in part, entering Ms. Pike's body."

Her lawyers had warned in advance that her small veins and a blood disorder called thrombocytosis would make IV placement especially difficult.

A pattern, not an outlier

This is the second failed execution attempt in Tennessee this year. In May, the aborted execution of Tony Carruthers was called off after the execution team failed to establish IV lines despite repeated attempts. Strada oversaw both.

Strada took over the Correction Department in January 2023 after serving as deputy director of the Arizona Department of Corrections — where he was a top official during three lethal injection executions later criticized for serious problems. In late 2024, he announced Tennessee was switching its protocol to pentobarbital only.

Gov. Lee has ordered an independent third-party review of the execution attempt and halted all executions in Tennessee for the rest of 2026. An interim commissioner will be announced later.

Pike's attorneys called the resignation "justified," adding: "What happened to her Wednesday reflects a systemic failure that goes far beyond any one person."
