41 Years, One Water Bottle: Arrest in the 1985 Murder of Hillary Hellewell

Forty-one years after 22-year-old Hillary L. Hellewell's body was found on a Newport, Rhode Island sidewalk, investigators say a discarded water bottle at a concert cracked the case.

William Morton, 61 — a former Newport man living in Smithfield — was arrested on Tuesday, Oct. 6, and arraigned in Newport District Court, charged with one count of first-degree murder. He is being held without bail and is scheduled for a status conference on Oct. 20, according to the Rhode Island Attorney General's Office. He remains presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty.

The night of May 19, 1985

Hellewell's body was found on the sidewalk in front of 25 Anthony St. in Newport at about 4:15 a.m. on May 19, 1985. Two brothers, Eric and Stephen Foss, were walking to their jobs with C&D Cleaners when they discovered her.

The medical examiner ruled the death a homicide: Hellewell had been strangled and sexually assaulted. On Sept. 24, prosecutors learned that a 2026 DNA profile from Morton was consistent with a 1985 DNA profile developed from Hellewell's pants and a blue T-shirt found near her body, Attorney General Peter F. Neronha announced.

The concert stakeout

Forensic investigators had been building toward a suspect through genetic genealogy — DNA from the clothing pointed to a family line, and additional work identified two men who were closely related to Morton through a paternal relative.

But investigators needed Morton's own DNA to confirm the match. "As soon as his name came up, any chance we had the ability to follow him, we followed him," said Michael Naylor, a cold case investigator in the attorney general's office who followed Morton to the Stadium Theatre in Woonsocket on Aug. 21, where Morton and his fiancée attended a concert by a Heart tribute band. When Morton left his water bottle on an empty seat, Naylor seized it.

The Cold Case Unit reopened the investigation in 2023 after the AG's office received funding for dedicated staff — a unit that has since documented more than 150 cold cases. Rhode Island didn't have DNA testing in 1985, but biological evidence had been carefully preserved — a retired Newport investigator, James Evan Hazel, reportedly carried the DNA sequence in his wallet for years. "So Newport never forgot about this case," Neronha said.

Neronha grew up in Jamestown and was born the same year as Hellewell; he said he had known her "at least from kindergarten" and called her "an amazing, fun, energetic person."

What comes next

The AG's office says the case will proceed to a grand jury. In a separate 1985 Newport case, Morton was convicted of sexually assaulting a 15-year-old, according to Rhode Island Supreme Court records cited by WJAR; his appeal was denied in 1989.
