GTA VI Is Hiding an Entire Second Game on Jason's Console

Most games hide a poster or a weapon behind a secret door. GTA VI hides a whole other video game — finished, playable, and long enough that beating it is an evening's work on its own.

This one is confirmed: Kotaku published a dedicated piece on the game, GameSpot has already put out a full puzzle guide for it, and streamers including Cellbit and ElBuni have played through it on camera.

Where it is. The JoySystem console in Jason's home. Boot it up and, alongside everything else, there is Tenebrae Lux — a complete, fully playable second game sitting inside GTA VI.

What it is. A black-and-white, gloomy, deliberately self-serious puzzle game. Its credits assign every single job — lead actor to music — to one name: Johannes Zuch, a fictional solo auteur. Kotaku reads the character as a jab at auteur game makers. Rockstar, for the record, has not said who Zuch is meant to be.

The puzzles. Grid puzzles, for a start. Then a looping room hiding a six-digit code — and here is the twist: the objects in the room differ from player to player, so the code can't just be copied out of a guide. And a bridge of glowing symbols that you cross by solving a poem. Rockstar hid a poetry exam inside a crime game.

The commitment. This is no five-minute Easter egg. A full run of Tenebrae Lux takes roughly two to three hours. If you get stuck, GameSpot's puzzle guide has the answers.

The Night Desk is tracking GTA VI's secrets as they verify. Leonida's hidden content usually pays in cash and cars; this one doesn't pay at all — it just sits there on Jason's console, daring you to spend your night inside a game inside a game.

Source: Kotaku, Tenebrae Lux piece (Nov. 2026); GameSpot, Tenebrae Lux puzzle guide; streamer playthroughs by Cellbit and ElBuni. This is guide and community intel collected by The Night Desk — not our own gameplay or discovery.
