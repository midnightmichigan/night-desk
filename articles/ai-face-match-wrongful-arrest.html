AI Got It Wrong, She Spent Six Months in Jail: TN Grandmother Sues Fargo for $10M Over Face-Match Arrest

By The Night Desk

Headline card by The Night Desk; Angela Lipps photo via NBC News; booking photo via A&E Networks

AI Got It Wrong, She Spent Six Months in Jail

A Tennessee grandmother was arrested at gunpoint while babysitting. She'd never been to the state where the crime happened. The case against her rested on a facial recognition match — and now it's a federal lawsuit.

Angela Robin Lipps, 50, of Elizabethton, Tennessee, was watching her neighbor's four young children one afternoon in July 2025 when U.S. Marshals surrounded the home and took her into custody at gunpoint. The charge: bank thefts in Fargo, North Dakota — a state she says she had never set foot in.

Lipps spent 108 days in a Tennessee county jail waiting to be extradited, then roughly two more months in a maximum-security facility in North Dakota on a $100,000 bond. The charges were finally dismissed in December 2025, after her attorney produced bank records proving she was in Tennessee when the frauds occurred. She was released on Christmas Eve — and, according to the complaint, lost her home, her vehicle, and most of her belongings in the months she was locked away.

How the wrong woman got flagged

According to the federal complaint, filed September 15, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of North Dakota, the whole thing started with fraud — someone else's.

Between April and May 2025, an unknown woman allegedly used stolen identities and fake identification cards to commit bank fraud at banks in Fargo and West Fargo. Investigators took a photograph from one of the fraudulent IDs — not surveillance footage — and ran it through Clearview AI, a facial recognition platform used by law enforcement.

The search surfaced Lipps' social media photo as a possible match.

Here's the part that makes the case: a West Fargo investigator who received that result reportedly declined to seek a warrant, recognizing that facial recognition results are investigative leads — not identifications — and that nothing else tied Lipps to the crimes.

The warrant went forward anyway

The complaint alleges Fargo Police Detective Lucas Heck picked the case up from there. It accuses him of:

  • Moving forward with charges knowing Lipps lived in Tennessee and had never been to North Dakota
  • Ignoring that she didn't match the physical description of the suspect on surveillance video
  • Having no unexplained funds connecting her to the thefts
  • Failing to disclose that the facial recognition result was based on a fake-ID photo, not surveillance footage — and that the software's results are meant to require independent corroboration

The physical differences, the lawsuit says, were obvious: different build, and Lipps has a distinctive neck tattoo the actual suspect reportedly didn't have.

Heck is named personally in the suit alongside the City of Fargo. The complaint alleges false arrest, malicious prosecution, and violations of Lipps' Fourth and Fourteenth Amendment rights. The family is seeking $10 million in damages. Her attorney, Eric Rice, put it plainly in court filings: the department was aware that unsupervised use of facial recognition "could result in the wrongful detention and prosecution of a subject" — and used it anyway.

The city has already said the quiet part

Fargo Mayor Tim Mahoney publicly apologized for what he called an "investigative mistake." That's an unusual admission, and it's on the record.

Experts told Fox News Digital this case is one of more than a dozen known instances of alleged police mishandling of facial recognition technology. Nate Freed-Wessler, deputy director of the American Civil Liberties Union, warned about what happens when flawed matching systems get treated as truth.

Think about what "verification" meant here: the software said maybe. One investigator said not enough. Another said good enough. A grandmother spent six months in jail on the difference.

The lawsuit is pending. Fargo officials told PEOPLE they don't comment on pending litigation.
