Krakow Main Square gunman dies in hospital after attack that killed six

The gunman is dead. The questions are not.

Michał Oksiński, 24, died at 4:30 a.m. Monday, authorities said. He had been in critical condition since Saturday's attack in Krakow's Main Square and had undergone several operations.

The attack

Wearing military-style clothing and a bulletproof vest, Oksiński drove into the main square in Krakow's Old Town on Saturday and opened fire. The square is a major tourist destination in Poland's second-largest city. Six people were killed and 15 wounded. Several foreigners were among the dead and wounded: those killed included citizens of Poland, the U.K. and Austria, and the wounded included people from Britain, Portugal, Russia, Germany and Austria.

Officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański were patrolling nearby. Police fired the first shot within three minutes of the initial report and chased the attacker as he fired a semiautomatic rifle. Kulik kept firing after he was shot himself. Both officers were seriously wounded; Kulik, 24, was in critical condition, prompting an urgent appeal for blood donors. A fundraiser for the officers and their families has raised more than 6 million zlotys (about $1.5 million), and Krakow's mayor announced both men will be named honorary citizens of the city. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called them the "real heroes."

A family found dead

Authorities also found the bodies of Oksiński's sister, mother and father at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 150 kilometers (93 miles) northeast of Krakow. Authorities said they believe Oksiński killed his family, possibly while they slept, before heading to Krakow.

His sister, Magdalena Oksińska, 21, was a third-year student at the Krakow Academy of Fine Arts. Among the victims in the square was Izabela Woźna, 53, a painter and mother of four who had opened an exhibition of her work in Krakow on Friday evening.

The motive investigation

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said Oksiński held "very radical" and "ultranationalistic" views. "We are first of all investigating if he was inspired by anyone, if someone helped him," Eryk Stasielak of the Krakow branch of the national prosecutor's office told The Associated Press. Investigators are still examining his motives and trying to access his electronic devices.

Prosecutors said a yellow flag bearing a black snake, known as the Gadsden flag, was found hanging on the wall of the attacker's room, and that authorities recovered 11 weapons from Oksiński, his car or his home, including two AR-15 semiautomatic rifles. Oksiński and his father held gun permits. He had trained at a local shooting club, which said he "was never trained in our club in the areas of dynamic, tactical or combat shooting."

A city in mourning

On Monday evening, people gathered in the Main Square for a silent march for the victims, placing candles where bullet marks were visible. President Karol Nawrocki and Prime Minister Tusk, otherwise political rivals, walked side by side. The night after the attack, St. Mary's Trumpet Call, the traditional five-note melody played hourly from the tower of St. Mary's Basilica, was paused for the first time since World War II.

Mass shootings are uncommon in Poland. In mid-October, three attacks at schools in the central province of Mazovia took place within four days, leaving one person dead and many wounded, including children.

Source: Associated Press reporting from Krakow, Poland.
