Halloween night in East Lansing — 5 wounded in two shootings off the MSU campus

Halloween night in East Lansing turned into one of the busiest nights the city has seen — two shootings, two separate drunken-driving crashes that injured pedestrians, and firefighters dispatched to 75 overnight fires, Police Chief Jennifer Brown said at a brief Sunday, Nov. 1 press conference.

The two shootings

After midnight, police were dispatched to M.A.C. Avenue and Linden Street, where they found three gunshot victims. Two are in stable condition, and Brown said she did not have the status of the third victim.

The second shooting happened in the early morning hours near Frye and Stoddard avenues. Two gunshot victims were taken to a local hospital, both listed in stable condition. Multiple guns were recovered at that scene — Brown said it has not yet been determined whether those were the weapons used in the shooting.

In all, five people were wounded. Four are in stable condition and the status of the fifth was unknown to police at the briefing.

"There's no known threat to the community at this time," Brown said. "The victims and their families are in our thoughts and prayers. We are aware that this situation was extremely frightening for bystanders, Michigan State University students as well as their families."

Brown emphasized that each crime scene was still "being worked" when the next incident began. She spoke for less than five minutes and did not take questions.

Two pedestrian crashes the same night

Before midnight, a vehicle struck a pedestrian near Abbot Road and Library Lane. A suspect was arrested for operating a vehicle while intoxicated. The pedestrian was initially in critical condition but was listed in stable condition after Sunday surgery.

Later, officers were dispatched to Grand River Avenue and Gunson Street for another pedestrian crash. Police arrested a driver for suspected drunken driving and recovered a firearm from the driver's vehicle. The pedestrian who was struck is in stable condition.

Brown said several people were arrested in total, including people involved in the two crashes.

MSU: off campus, no one affiliated

Michigan State University President Kevin Guskiewicz said in a letter to the MSU community that the shootings took place off campus and did not involve anyone affiliated with MSU.

"The safety of our students, faculty and staff remains our priority," he wrote in a Nov. 1 post.

Brown was joined at the briefing by East Lansing Fire Chief John Newman, Michigan State Police First Lt. Jay Barkley, Mayor Erik Altmann and Councilman Steve Whelan, though none of the others spoke.

Still developing

Police have not released the victims' identities, said whether the two shootings are connected, or announced any shooting arrests. The Night Desk will update this article as East Lansing police release more.

Facts in this article are drawn from the Lansing State Journal's November 1 report on Chief Brown's press conference and President Guskiewicz's letter to the MSU community. Victim identities and any connection between the two shootings had not been released as of that report.
